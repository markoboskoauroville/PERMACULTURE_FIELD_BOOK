# The Patient Ground

**A Mantra field book on permaculture.**

Live: https://markoboskoauroville.github.io/PERMACULTURE_FIELD_BOOK/
Plain text: [BOOK.md](BOOK.md)

Where permaculture came from, what it actually says, **what the evidence now
shows**, and how the standard advice breaks on Mediterranean karst. Twelve
chapters, thirteen plates.

## The two chapters that make this different

**Chapter ten — Does it actually work?** Most permaculture writing asserts.
This one cites: Ferguson & Lovell (2014) on the movement's isolation from
science, Krebs & Bach (2018) on the twelve principles, and Reiff et al.
(2024), the first large-scale yield study — eleven Central European sites,
production comparable to conventional and organic. The result is reported as
what it is: good news, modestly sized, not a vindication of everything.

**Chapter nine — Karst, bora and fire.** Swales are usually wrong on
limestone. Windbreaks must be permeable. Autumn planting, not spring. Fire is
a design condition, not a footnote.

## Chapter eleven contains no numbers, on purpose

The Kukljica EcoOasis chapter gives the design *questions* and no site data —
no plot size, rainfall, cistern volume, soil depth or tree inventory —
because the survey does not exist yet and inventing plausible figures would
be worse than leaving the gap. The chapter says this inside itself. When the
survey exists, rewrite it with real numbers.

Ends with **What I could not verify**. Not optional.
